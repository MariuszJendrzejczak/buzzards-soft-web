# Research — elevator pitch / professional summary principles (for the main hero)

> Web research requested by the owner (2026-07-25) to ground the main recruiter-facing
> hero rewrite. Question: *what are the rules of an effective elevator pitch / résumé
> summary for a developer — what it must contain, what to avoid, and what form (length,
> tone, structure) it should take.* Multi-source, cross-checked. Sources listed at the
> end; each finding cites where it converges.

## TL;DR (the 8 rules that matter here)

1. **A pitch sells VALUE, not a job description.** Lead with the outcome/value the
   reader gets, not a list of what you do or what tools you know. (hero best-practice +
   résumé-summary guidance both say this.)
2. **Form: 2–4 sentences, ~75 words, 30–60 seconds spoken.** Every word earns its place.
3. **The 3 C's: Clarity · Conciseness · Confidence.** Plain language, active voice,
   power verbs, no hedging.
4. **Structure (ordered): identity + seniority → specialization → proof → goal/value →
   call to action.**
5. **Specificity IS credibility.** The single biggest lever. Concrete scope + method +
   (where possible) metric beats any adjective. Vague claims make recruiters doubt the
   *rest* of the résumé too.
6. **Kill buzzwords & clichés.** "passionate, results-driven, team player, innovative,
   responsible for" — 73% recruiter fatigue; they read as empty.
7. **AI positioning: show APPLIED, specific workflow + outcome — never "I use AI".**
   Naming the concrete technique/toolchain (agentic workflow, own config) is what makes
   it credible instead of hype.
8. **End on a concrete next step** (the ask). A pitch without a CTA is incomplete.

## 1. What an elevator pitch / hero actually is (its job)

- A hero/pitch must **immediately answer "what's in it for me?"** for the reader —
  prioritise a clear value proposition over "telling visitors what you do" (Prismic;
  Marketer Milk).
- On a page, **~8 of 10 people read only the headline**; ~2 of 10 read the rest — so the
  headline carries the value, the sub-line supports it with detail/benefit (OptinMonster;
  Landingrabbit).
- It should **spark interest and invite a conversation**, tailored to a specific audience
  (Asana; Indeed; UPenn GSC).

## 2. Form — length, tone, voice

- **Length:** résumé professional summary = **2–4 sentences**; spoken elevator pitch =
  **30–60 s ≈ ~75 words** for the 30-s version. Limit to **≤3 key points** (Enhancv;
  Indeed).
- **The 3 C's — Clarity, Conciseness, Confidence** (Indeed; Northeastern):
  - *Clarity* — plain language; the reader grasps background + intent instantly.
  - *Conciseness* — every word serves a purpose; tailor to audience.
  - *Confidence* — positive, active, not rehearsed-sounding; practise from bullet points,
    not a memorised script.
- **Active voice + power verbs** ("developed, implemented, optimized, deployed, built");
  **no passive / hedging** (Enhancv; ResumeWorded; AI-skills sources).

## 3. Structure — the ordered components

Convergent 4–5 part skeleton (Indeed elevator-pitch + Enhancv résumé-summary):

1. **Identity + seniority** — who you are + years/level, stated early
   ("*[title] with [X years] in [specialty]*").
2. **Specialization / expertise** — the specific stack/domain you own (gives instant
   technical clarity).
3. **Proof** — one quantifiable or concrete achievement (the credibility anchor).
4. **Goal + value** — what you want and the value you bring to *this* reader.
5. **Call to action** — a concrete next step.

## 4. What to INCLUDE (the credibility levers)

- **Specificity over adjectives — this is the #1 rule.** Replace vague language with
  **scope + method + metric** (e.g. *"Cut churn 18% using Python and Tableau"*). Vague
  buzzwords "make you seem less credible, which makes savvy recruiters question the other
  claims on your résumé" (LiveCareer; Teal; ResumeWorded).
- **Position as someone who drives outcomes through technical work** — not a language
  list. "Show how your engineering decisions create value / solve real problems"
  (TealHQ; Jobscan).
- **Name seniority, specialization, and a signature capability** early (Enhancv).
- **One concrete differentiator** beats three generic strengths (Rule of ≤3 points).

## 5. What to AVOID

- **Buzzwords / clichés:** *passionate, results-driven, team player, innovative,
  responsible for, detail-oriented, go-getter, synergy.* 73% of recruiters report
  fatigue; they read as vague and meaningless (LiveCareer; Novoresume; CareerEnlightenment).
- **Vague claims without support**, generic language that fits any engineer, passive
  constructions (Enhancv).
- **Jargon overload / too many details** — cap at ≤3 points; don't cram (Indeed;
  Northeastern).
- **Unearned superlatives / hype** — "best", "revolutionary" without proof erode trust.
- **A weak or missing call to action.**

## 6. AI-native positioning — the credible way (directly relevant to this hero)

The strongest, most current guidance (2026 sources) — and the biggest trap to avoid:

- **Never just "experienced with AI" / "proficient in ChatGPT".** That "reads like
  claiming you're proficient in Google search" (AI Native Student).
- **Show APPLIED use + a specific technique/workflow + an outcome.** Prompt/agentic work
  is credible "only when paired with a specific technique and a project that shows you
  actually used it" (AI Native Student; Underdog.io agentic-AI guide).
- **Demonstrate you understand how autonomous systems fit inside a larger workflow** —
  the senior signal, not tool-name-dropping (Agilemania; Underdog.io).
- **Connect AI to outcomes** (speed, scope, reliability), backed by specifics
  (Resume-now; InterviewGuys).
- **Implication for this hero:** the owner's real differentiator — *Claude Code with his
  own configuration, hooks, skills, agents in the loop, daily, with a human owning
  decisions/quality* — is EXACTLY the specific, applied, workflow-level signal the
  research says is credible. The job is to state it concretely, not to reach for "AI
  elevates me" abstractions.

## 7. Value-proposition formulas (adapt, don't copy)

- **"I help (X) do (Y) by doing (Z)"** — target + outcome + unique method (Steve Blank;
  Copyhackers). For a personal brand: *X = the team/role, Y = the outcome, Z = your
  AI-native method.*
- **"The only/best/easiest X for Y"** (who-specific) — offer + audience (Copyhackers).
- **Hero adaptation:** headline = the value/outcome hook (what the reader gets);
  sub-line = the proof + method (how). Keep the headline graspable in seconds.

## 8. The key tension for THIS hero — and how to resolve it

**Research says:** specificity/metrics are the #1 credibility lever; vagueness makes the
whole thing doubted.
**Owner preference (this project):** *"don't justify yourself in the pitch — proof is in
the portfolio"* ([[pitch-asserts-portfolio-proves]]).

These are **not** in conflict once separated:

- **"Don't justify" ≠ "stay vague".** Justifying = defensively arguing *why you're
  credible* ("I can prove it, here's why…"). Specificity = stating a **concrete fact**
  plainly ("4 years in Flutter/Unity; I rebuilt my workflow around agentic AI"). The
  first is defensive; the second is confident. Research's credibility lever is the
  **second**, which the owner rule does *not* forbid.
- **Resolution — division of labour:**
  - **Headline** = the value/outcome hook, punchy, graspable in seconds (the 8-of-10 read).
  - **Sub-line** = 2–3 sentences carrying the **concrete specifics** (seniority, exact
    stack, the signature AI-native workflow) stated as fact, not defence — plus the ask.
  - **Deep proof (repos, portfolio, `/#warsztat`)** stays in the portfolio, reached via
    the secondary CTA. That is where "proof lives", per the owner rule.
  - **The full backstory / arc lives in the "About me" section, NOT the hero** (owner,
    2026-07-25): *"here = elevation, there = details."* So the hero must stay a short
    elevation — the evolution narrative (experienced mobile dev → AI-native fullstack)
    belongs to the About section; the hero only carries the value hook + a minimal
    concrete spine + the ask. This makes the hero LIGHTER than the earlier drafts, which
    were trying to tell the whole arc in the sub.
- **Net:** be **specific and confident**, not **defensive or vague**. Specific facts are
  the pitch's spine; arguing for them is what we cut.

## 9. Applied blueprint for the main hero (structure, not final words)

Mapped onto the existing keys (`badges`, `badgeSub`, `hero.heading*`, `hero.sub`,
`ctaPrimary/Secondary`):

| `badgeSub` | seniority + one concrete signal | "4+ lata… AI codziennie" (already there) |
| **`hero.heading`** | **the value/outcome hook** — 1 idea, graspable in seconds, no cliché | the elevator-pitch punch (value the reader gets) |
| **`hero.sub`** | 2–3 sentences: concrete specifics (stack + signature AI workflow) as fact + the goal | the credibility spine + the ask |
| `ctaPrimary` | the call to action | "Porozmawiajmy o roli lub projekcie" (already there) |
| `ctaSecondary` | route to deep proof (portfolio) | "Zobacz, jak pracuję" (already there) |

**Design implications for the next copy attempt:**
1. Headline states the **value/outcome**, not "I evolved" and not "I use AI". (Evolution
   is context, not the hook.)
2. Sub carries **one specific, concrete method-signal** (the agentic workflow), stated as
   fact, plus the goal — no defensive justification, no metric invented.
3. ≤3 points total across heading+sub; ruthless word economy (~75-word feel).
4. Zero buzzwords; active voice; confident, not hyped.
5. Keep the CTA + portfolio route doing the "proof" job.

## Revision 2026-07-25 — nomenclature (fullstack / web / frontend / backend) + honest side-project framing

Follow-up research after the owner flagged that "web fullstack" grates, because his web
work is on **his own projects**, not commercial for clients.

### What the terms actually mean (convergent, multi-source)
- **"Full stack" is a WEB term.** It means frontend **+** backend of a **web application**:
  client-side (UI) + server-side (logic, databases, APIs). A full-stack dev "can build
  complete web applications independently, both sides" (freeCodeCamp; Indeed; Talent500).
- **Frontend** = client-side / UI (HTML/CSS/JS, React; Flutter is also classed as a
  client/UI tech). **Backend** = servers, databases, APIs, security, logic.
- **"Full stack" ≠ "knows everything"** — it = understands how frontend + backend + DB fit
  together and can ship both sides (freeCodeCamp).
- Consequence: calling mobile work "full stack" is a category error — full-stack is about
  **web** front+back. Mobile (Flutter/Unity) is its own axis.

### Fact correction (owner, 2026-07-25) — precision issue (Voice-Gap-Test dim 11)
- **Commercial experience = mobile** (Flutter/Dart, Unity/C#).
- **Web work is on HIS OWN projects** (e.g. this very site: Next.js / React / TypeScript /
  Firebase, incl. a Cloud-Function backend) — **not** paid client delivery.
- So **"Web Fullstack Developer" / "web fullstack" as a bare professional claim implies
  commercial web experience → it overstates.** The badge asserts a title; the prose must
  not present personal web work as commercial.

### Honest-framing rules (multi-source: résumé/side-project guidance)
- Only claim skills you can **discuss confidently**; the interview surfaces the truth.
- **Label personal / side projects distinctly** from professional experience. Side projects
  are **legitimate proof of capability** — but not the same as commercial delivery.
- Show concrete work, not broad self-labels.

### Resolution — the owner's own instinct is the best answer
Frame web as an **AI-enabled STACK EXPANSION done on his own builds**, not as commercial
web experience. This is simultaneously:
- **Accurate** — separates commercial mobile from own-project web.
- **Credible** — matches the applied-AI rule (§6) + the side-project honesty rule.
- **Elevating** — growth + initiative + AI-as-multiplier extending his range.
- **The concrete cash-out of the ORIGINAL brief** ("współpraca z AI podnosi moje
  możliwości") — this is finally the honest, specific form: *AI let me extend my stack
  from mobile into web.* The elevation the owner wanted, grounded in a real fact.

**Phrasing directions:**
- Plain: *"komercyjnie robię mobile (Flutter, Unity); z agentami AI rozszerzyłem swój stack
  o web — frontend i backend — na własnych projektach."*
- Keep "fullstack" only if qualified as own-projects / a capability, never as client work.

### Open questions for the owner (needed for precise copy)
1. **Scope of the web work** — frontend only, or frontend + backend? Which stack
   (Next.js / TypeScript / Firebase)? (This site suggests frontend + a Cloud-Function backend.)
2. **The "Web Fullstack Developer" badge** — keep (as a proven *capability*), soften, or
   requalify given web is personal?
3. **Adopt the AI-enabled-stack-expansion framing** in the hero? (Recommended.)
